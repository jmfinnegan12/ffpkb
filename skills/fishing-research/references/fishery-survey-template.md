# Template — Regional Fishery Survey

For "where to fish in <region> during <window>" reports. An open survey of a drainage/region's waters, organized by access effort. This is the structure built for a Fernie/Elk Valley report; reuse it.

## Research angles (one subagent each)

1. **Species** — species present (abundance/size/status). For regulations, return only the links to the official rules, plus any closure or gear rule that rules a water out or changes the technique for the window.
2. **Fisheries science** — recent population/monitoring studies, threats (contaminants, disease, thermal), status changes. Peer-reviewed vs agency vs media, dated.
3. **Techniques + guide/shop patterns** — how the water is actually fished, hatch/forage timing for the window, fly patterns, and any recent shifts in tactics. Verify techniques against gear rules.
4. **Waters — roadside + day-hike tiers** — enumerate specific waters, species/quality, access/effort, window-specific notes.
5. **Waters — multi-day backcountry tier** — the same for waters needing an overnight; access logistics, permits, hazards.

(Adjust angle count to the region; keep species/regs and science separate from waters.)

## Report structure

**Frontmatter + meta line** — region, window, prepared date, one-line framing.

**How to read / tier legend** — the three access tiers defined; a blunt orientation line (e.g. does backcountry actually mean better fish here, or just solitude?).

**Part I — General context (applies across all tiers)** — so it isn't repeated per water:
- The fishery at a glance (geography, headline species, what the window means)
- **Species table** — species · native? · status · abundance/size · angler rule
- **Seasonal picture for the window** — runoff/flow/temps; a hatch/forage calendar
- **Closures — split explicitly into (a) FIXED seasonal closures and (b) DISCRETIONARY in-season heat/drought orders.** Never present a discretionary order as a standing rule. State what applies to the window and what to check before travel.
- **Before you go** — links to the official rules, emergency rules, and gauges; no regulation text (`wiki-page-format.md`)
- **Watershed / fishery health** — contaminants, disease, thermal, population framing; correct any commonly-conflated narratives
- **Techniques & trends** — cross-tier method notes; late/early-season tactics; verified against gear rules
- **Safety** — bears, water temps/fish-welfare, remoteness, road hazards

**Part II — Tier 1: Roadside / easy access** — by water body: species & quality · access & effort · window-specific seasonal notes. Include the region's anchor water here even if backcountry is the trip's goal.

**Part III — Tier 2: Day-hike backcountry** — same per-water structure. Be honest about barren/fishless waters.

**Part IV — Tier 3: Multi-day backpack backcountry** — same, plus access logistics, permits/allocations, and hazards.

**Part V — At-a-glance comparison table** — water × tier × species × window verdict × access × hazard. Verdicts weigh fish quality/numbers, access/effort, crowding, and window conditions — never a rule matching the angler's own practices, such as catch-and-release-only or single-fly-only, against the water (quality-bar §6).

**Sources & data-quality notes** — strong vs thin evidence; conflicts surfaced; anything left `unverified`; dated sources flagged; contradictions the self-critique caught.

**Related reading** — wikilinks to related vault reports (species profiles for the region's fish, adjacent regional surveys).

## Per-water rule

Every water gets its **window-specific** seasonal note — not a generic line. Runoff done? Water temps? Which hatches? Any closure (fixed or likely-discretionary) touching the window?

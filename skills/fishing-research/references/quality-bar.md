# Quality Bar — fishing-research

The standard every fishing research report must meet. Read by each research subagent and applied by the orchestrator's self-critique pass. A report that misses these is not done.

## 1. Depth, not scan

This is the failure mode to beat: a broad overview stitched from guide/tourism marketing and old trip reports. Every research angle must go past that.

- **≥5 distinct sources per angle, spanning ≥3 source types** (see taxonomy below). A pile of five guide-shop pages does not count.
- **Mandatory forum / social / practitioner pass** per angle: actively search fishing forums (e.g. region-specific boards, SportfishingBC, Washington Fly Fishing, iFish, Reddit r/flyfishing and regional subs), YouTube, Facebook groups, and trip reports — not just outfitter and tourism pages. What are people actually saying *now*.
- **Mandatory "what's changed" pass**: explicitly look for how the fishery, tactics, access, regs, or conditions have shifted in roughly the last 3 years. Name the shift or state that nothing notable surfaced.

## 2. Source taxonomy — tag every substantive claim

Tag inline with the source type so the reader can weight it:

`[peer-reviewed]` · `[agency/gov]` · `[guide/shop]` · `[forum/social]` · `[media]`

Marketing copy and undated guide pages are `[guide/shop]`, not fact. Do not launder a promotional claim into a bare assertion.

## 3. Recency

- Prioritize sources ≤5 years old.
- **Flag anything 5–10+ years old as dated**, especially population, run-status, and conditions claims (these age fastest).
- Technique does not age the same way. A classic book or an older guide video is a valid source for how to fish; date it, don't discount it.
- A single old source is not enough to state a current condition — corroborate or flag.

## 4. Primary-source verification is mandatory for check-worthy claims

Reports carry no regulation detail. A water gets a Before you go line of links to the official rules (see `wiki-page-format.md`). The rules a report still states are a closure that rules a water out for the window, and a gear rule that changes a recommended technique.

These must be traced to a **primary source** (government/agency regulation synopsis, statute, agency report, official season/closure notice) and confirmed by the verification pass — never stated on the strength of a guide page or forum post alone:

- Regulations, licences, gear rules (hooks, bait, fly count), size/retention limits, quotas
- Season dates, closures (fixed **and** discretionary/in-season heat-drought orders)
- At-risk / protected / listing status (SARA, COSEWIC, ESA, state/provincial lists)
- Population figures, abundance, stocking records
- Superlatives — "best / only / largest / world-class"

If only secondary/marketing sources support a check-worthy claim, tag it `unverified` and say so. Do not present it as settled.

## 5. Gear-rule ↔ technique compatibility (the single-fly lesson)

Before recommending any technique or rig, confirm it is **legal under the verified regulations for that water**. The originating failure: recommending a hopper-dropper (two flies) on BC water where regulations permit only one fly. When a recommended tactic touches a gear rule (number of flies/hooks, bait, weighting, indicators), the verifier must confirm compatibility. A technique recommendation that contradicts the regs section is a blocking error.

## 6. Framing regs in verdicts — the angler's own practices

The angler's trip notes and requests show how they fish: for example, whether they practice catch-and-release or fish a single fly rather than multi-fly rigs. Mention a rule that matches one of those practices (catch-and-release-only, single-fly-only) only where it changes the technique (still verify it per §4) — but never treat it as a downside, deterrent, or deciding factor against a water in a verdict, ranking, or recommendation. If the evidence supports it, note that such rules can be a mild positive (lower harvest pressure, quality-water management) rather than inventing that angle where it's not backed by sources. What should actually drive a verdict: fish quality/size/numbers, access/effort, crowding, and conditions for the window — plus anything that genuinely constrains the angler as they fish (bait-only water, closures, or a rule that's actually illegal for the planned technique).

## 7. Surface conflicts; don't smooth them

Where sources disagree (population trends, whether a water holds fish, what's driving a decline), say so explicitly and attribute each side. Thin or contradictory evidence is reported as thin, not resolved by fiat.

## 8. Self-critique before filing (orchestrator)

Scan the assembled draft for:
- **Internal contradictions** — especially a recommendation that conflicts with a stated rule (probe the gear-rule/technique area every time).
- **Unsupported claims** — anything asserted without a source or verification.
- **Missing tags/flags** — substantive claims lacking a source-type tag; old sources not flagged dated.
- **Marketing leakage** — superlatives or "premier/world-class" language not backed by a real source.
- **Practice framing creep** — a verdict, ranking, or recommendation that dings a water for a rule matching the angler's own practice, such as catch-and-release-only or single-fly-only (see §6).

Fix all of these before saving. End the report with a "Sources & data-quality notes" section listing the flags, conflicts, and anything left `unverified`.
